# Editorial stance and daily reading shape

## Confessional premise

This is a private Christian Bible study built on the premise that the Bible is true and divinely inspired. The app does not present that premise as the result of a source-count vote, and it does not hide it behind artificial neutrality.

The starting interpretive posture is the ordinary grammatical, literary, and historical sense of the received text. Unless wording, genre, context, or canonical use gives good reason to recognize metaphor, symbolism, hyperbole, typology, or another figure, the study treats what the text affirms as real. “Literal” must not become “literalistic”: poetry remains poetry, a metaphor remains meaningful as a metaphor, and a writer's communicative purpose matters more than flattening every sentence into the same genre.

## Supernatural claims and evidentiary weight

Scripture's own presentation of prophecy, miracle, divine action, and named authorship is the study's working presumption. Methodological naturalism is not a neutral baseline. An argument such as “the prediction must have been written after the event because predictive prophecy is impossible” supplies no independent evidence against the text's claim; the same is true of rejecting a miracle merely because it is miraculous.

Displacing an explicit biblical claim or a well-established traditional attribution therefore requires very strong positive evidence—textual, manuscript, linguistic, or historical—that does not depend on first excluding supernatural revelation. Abrupt literary transitions, a fulfilled prediction, polished language, or a reconstructed social setting may deserve investigation, but none automatically establishes later authorship or redaction. The synthesis must separate observable evidence from the philosophical assumptions used to interpret it and must say when a reconstruction remains a reconstruction.

This weighting is asymmetric but not incurious. Secular and historical-critical scholarship can be excellent for language, geography, material culture, social history, chronology, literary relationships, and for identifying questions a confessional reader should answer. It is contextual evidence or a serious counterposition, not an authority that overrules Scripture simply because its method excludes divine action. Influential contrary theories should be steel-manned in the deep study when they materially affect interpretation, but the daily article need not center them merely because they are common in modern academic literature.

Traditional explanations remain accountable to evidence. A confessional premise does not turn every inherited harmonization into fact, erase genuine textual difficulty, or permit weak historical claims. It does determine where the burden of proof lies and prevents an anti-supernatural assumption from masquerading as a conclusion.

## Fair engagement and conclusions

Influential or widespread alternative readings and objections receive their strongest reasonable formulation, best evidence, and real limitations. The synthesis then states why the study finds one reading more persuasive when the source set and text permit that judgment. It does not imply that every position is equally strong, catalog every fringe claim, manufacture consensus, or dismiss a view merely because it arises from a different tradition.

Drafting should keep four layers visible:

1. What the biblical text directly says or structurally presents.
2. What follows by close exegetical inference.
3. What follows from broader canonical or theological synthesis.
4. What belongs to a historical, scientific, or philosophical coordination model.

Uncertainty is stated where evidence is genuinely underdetermined. A confessional starting point does not license invented facts, weak citations, selective quotation, or claims that the consulted sources do not support.

Wayne Grudem's *Systematic Theology* is currently Dustin's example of the desired steelman-and-assess method; it is not listed as a consulted source or used for substantive claims unless a lawful, exact edition is later supplied and recorded in the source registry.

## Audience and prose standard

Write for two readers with doctoral-level scientific training and extensive familiarity with Scripture, theology, and serious secondary literature. Expertise should permit directness, not produce compressed or performative prose. Use plain, precise language. Keep a technical term only when the term itself matters to the argument, and explain it briefly when ordinary English would not make the point equally well. Do not rehearse the passage they have just read, but do supply enough local context for each written unit to make sense on its own.

The prose should be substantial without feeling crowded. Prefer concrete verbs, varied sentence length, natural transitions, and one main movement per paragraph. Delete throat-clearing, repeated conclusions, generic devotional language, exhaustive commentator-by-commentator reporting, vocabulary used mainly to signal sophistication, and strings of abstract nouns that obscure the claim. A sentence earns its place by clarifying the text, resolving a real interpretive question, supplying useful context, testing a theological conclusion, or showing a concrete consequence.

Every displayed unit—the orientation, each main-summary paragraph, the practical takeaway, and each expanded section—must stand alone in the context of that day's Scripture. Do not rely on another app section, a prior day's commentary, or directions such as “as noted above” and “see below.” Each unit should also make its interpretive or practical payoff clear. Technical material need not end in a devotional command, but the reader should be able to tell why the observation changes understanding, judgment, worship, or action.

## Daily page hierarchy

The default reading path should be short enough for daily use:

1. **Today's orientation:** one or two clear paragraphs, no more than 150 words.
2. **Scripture:** the unmodified ESV chapter through the server-side provider adapter; omitted on a book-introduction day.
3. **Commentary summary:** one coherent, source-grounded argument in continuous prose. Its length follows the material rather than an arbitrary point count; 250–600 words is a useful norm and 700 words is the validation ceiling. It should read comfortably at daily-reading pace, use specialist vocabulary selectively, and state the practical or interpretive stakes without becoming a catalogue or sermon outline. Numerical citations sit immediately after the claims they support rather than being grouped at paragraph ends.
4. **Verse of the day:** one representative verse selected from that day's configured chapter or multi-chapter reading. Store only its book/chapter/verse reference in commentary metadata. Page 3 derives the exact, unmodified wording from the same live ESV response used on Page 2 and displays provider attribution; it never stores a duplicate verse in commentary or substitutes another translation.
5. **Practical takeaway:** one concrete, specific action or diagnostic for the present day, expressed in one sentence of no more than 32 words. Avoid generic imperatives that could follow any passage.
6. **Comprehensive synthesis and research audit:** one passage-shaped source document displayed as a small set of custom, separately collapsed sections, followed by a separately collapsed coverage audit and full source list.

Every notable commentary claim in the orientation, main synthesis, and takeaway carries source IDs tied to the exact consulted records that support it. The verse selection is sourced directly to its biblical reference and live ESV response rather than to a commentary record. The main synthesis and takeaway use inline numerical citations linked to one numbered list, keeping the prose readable on a phone without weakening the audit trail. Citations should help the reader audit the claim, not decorate it. Inaccessible works, search snippets, and bibliographic leads cannot support a displayed claim.

The mobile interface divides this hierarchy into three comfortable reading pages. Page 1 contains the orientation. Page 2 contains the ESV chapter; on a dedicated book-introduction day, which intentionally has no chapter, it contains the book overview instead. Page 3 contains the unified commentary summary, the selected live ESV verse beside the concrete takeaway, and numbered source notes. The same reading-level comment form appears beneath each visible page. The optional comprehensive synthesis and research audit come after the discussion and page navigation so opening them never moves the comment box far below a long essay.

## Genesis 1 calibration (preserved)

For this pilot, the ordinary-day reading is the starting assessment because the numbered *yom*, evening-and-morning refrain, sequence, and later Sabbath analogy give it a substantial natural claim. Framework, analogical, day-age, functional, and other influential readings are still presented from their strongest consulted sources and evaluated on their actual arguments. Literary structure, ancient cosmology, and modern scientific coordination are not assumed either to overturn or to prove the ordinary reading by themselves.

The Genesis calibration remains private historical project material. Dustin subsequently authorized the narrowly bounded Celebration bridge: seven scheduled reading IDs, substantive synthesis only for days 54–56, and explicit placeholders for days 57–60. This does not authorize bulk generation or a complete new chronology.
