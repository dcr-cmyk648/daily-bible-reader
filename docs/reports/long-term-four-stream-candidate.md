## Stream order and confidence

### old testament

1. Genesis — Genesis opens the plan because its creation, fall, flood, and patriarchal narrative supplies the canonical and historical frame for the remaining Bible. (high confidence)
2. Job — Job is placed after Genesis as an early patriarchal-era possibility, while its setting and composition remain too disputed for a firmer claim. (low confidence)
3. Exodus — Exodus resumes the Pentateuch's narrated sequence after the patriarchal setting provisionally associated with Job. (medium confidence)
4. Leviticus — Leviticus continues Sinai legislation without separating it from Exodus. (high confidence)
5. Numbers — Numbers continues Israel's wilderness journey after Sinai. (high confidence)
6. Deuteronomy — Deuteronomy concludes Moses' wilderness addresses immediately before entry into Canaan. (high confidence)
7. Joshua — Joshua follows Deuteronomy's entry-and-conquest setting. (high confidence)
8. Judges — Judges follows the settlement era narrated in Joshua. (high confidence)
9. Ruth — Ruth is located in the judges period named by its opening setting. (medium confidence)
10. 1 Samuel — 1 Samuel moves from the judges era into Samuel, Saul, and David. (high confidence)
11. 2 Samuel — 2 Samuel continues David's reign from 1 Samuel. (high confidence)
12. Song of Solomon — Song of Solomon is provisionally placed near the united monarchy by its Solomonic association; dating and authorship remain disputed. (low confidence)
13. Ecclesiastes — Ecclesiastes follows Song as another wisdom book traditionally associated with Solomon, without claiming a settled composition date. (low confidence)
14. 1 Kings — 1 Kings resumes the united monarchy and then the divided kingdom after David. (high confidence)
15. 2 Kings — 2 Kings continues the divided kingdoms through the exile. (high confidence)
16. 1 Chronicles — 1 Chronicles retells the monarchy after Kings; its placement preserves the historical sequence while acknowledging its later shaping. (medium confidence)
17. 2 Chronicles — 2 Chronicles continues the Chronicler's retelling through exile and the Cyrus decree. (medium confidence)
18. Jonah — Jonah is placed early among the prophets because its named Nineveh mission is commonly associated with the eighth-century Assyrian period. (medium confidence)
19. Amos — Amos follows in the eighth-century northern-kingdom setting specified in its superscription. (medium confidence)
20. Hosea — Hosea remains with the late northern-kingdom crisis reflected in its royal notices. (medium confidence)
21. Isaiah — Isaiah is placed with Judah's eighth-century kings, while later portions and compositional questions remain acknowledged rather than settled. (medium confidence)
22. Micah — Micah is paired with the same eighth-century Judah setting named in its opening. (medium confidence)
23. Nahum — Nahum is placed after the fall of Thebes and before Nineveh's fall, the historical interval normally inferred from the book's own allusion. (medium confidence)
24. Zephaniah — Zephaniah is placed in Josiah's reign before the Babylonian crisis. (medium confidence)
25. Habakkuk — Habakkuk is provisionally located as Babylon rises against Judah, a setting inferred from its announced Chaldean threat. (medium confidence)
26. Jeremiah — Jeremiah follows through Judah's final decades and Jerusalem's fall. (high confidence)
27. Lamentations — Lamentations follows Jerusalem's destruction because its poems mourn that catastrophe. (high confidence)
28. Ezekiel — Ezekiel continues the exilic setting from the Babylonian deportation. (high confidence)
29. Daniel — Daniel is kept in the exile-to-Persia sequence of its narrative setting while authorship and final-form dating remain disputed. (low confidence)
30. Obadiah — Obadiah is provisionally placed after Jerusalem's fall because its Edom oracle is often read in that setting, though its date is debated. (low confidence)
31. Joel — Joel remains a deliberately provisional late placement because internal dating evidence is disputed. (low confidence)
32. Haggai — Haggai is placed in the early Persian restoration by its dated oracles. (high confidence)
33. Zechariah — Zechariah follows Haggai in the early restoration era indicated by its dated visions and temple context. (high confidence)
34. Ezra — Ezra follows the restoration prophets as a narrative of return, temple rebuilding, and reform. (medium confidence)
35. Esther — Esther is placed in the Persian-diaspora period between the return narratives without treating its precise chronology as settled. (medium confidence)
36. Nehemiah — Nehemiah follows Ezra as the later Persian-period rebuilding and reform account. (medium confidence)
37. Malachi — Malachi closes the OT stream as a post-exilic prophetic voice, though its precise date remains approximate. (medium confidence)

### new testament

1. Matthew — Matthew begins the NT stream with Jesus' life and commission. (high confidence)
2. Mark — Mark remains alongside the Gospel narratives before the church's expansion in Acts. (high confidence)
3. Luke — Luke precedes Acts because the two-volume narrative explicitly continues from Jesus' ministry to the church. (high confidence)
4. John — John remains with the Gospel witness to Jesus before Acts. (high confidence)
5. Acts — Acts completes the narrative bridge from the risen Christ's commission to the apostolic mission. (high confidence)
6. James — James leads the letters as a traditional early-Jewish-Christian correspondence placement; exact dating remains debated. (medium confidence)
7. Galatians — Galatians follows as an early Pauline letter in this pragmatic composition-aware sequence. (medium confidence)
8. 1 Thessalonians — 1 Thessalonians is placed among the earliest widely dated Pauline letters. (medium confidence)
9. 2 Thessalonians — 2 Thessalonians follows its paired Thessalonian correspondence, while relative dating remains debated. (medium confidence)
10. 1 Corinthians — 1 Corinthians begins the Corinthian correspondence in the middle Pauline mission period. (medium confidence)
11. 2 Corinthians — 2 Corinthians follows its linked Corinthian correspondence. (medium confidence)
12. Romans — Romans follows the Corinthian letters as a mature Pauline letter commonly connected with the end of that mission phase. (medium confidence)
13. Colossians — Colossians begins the prison-letter grouping in this traditional composition-aware proposal. (medium confidence)
14. Philemon — Philemon remains adjacent to Colossians because both are traditionally associated with the same imprisonment and personnel. (medium confidence)
15. Ephesians — Ephesians follows within the traditional prison-letter grouping; authorship and date are marked as debated rather than resolved. (medium confidence)
16. Philippians — Philippians completes this provisional prison-letter grouping. (medium confidence)
17. 1 Timothy — 1 Timothy begins the Pastoral sequence in its traditional post-mission placement. (medium confidence)

19. 2 Timothy — 2 Timothy follows as the traditional final Pauline testament; exact reconstruction remains debated. (medium confidence)
20. Hebrews — Hebrews follows the Pauline/Pastoral group as an early Christian homily-letter whose authorship is unresolved. (medium confidence)
21. 1 Peter — 1 Peter leads the Petrine letters in their traditional apostolic association. (medium confidence)
22. 2 Peter — 2 Peter follows 1 Peter as the paired Petrine witness while date and authorship remain disputed. (medium confidence)
23. Jude — Jude precedes the Johannine letters because of its traditional early-Christian placement and literary relation to 2 Peter. (medium confidence)
24. 1 John — 1 John begins the Johannine letters before its shorter companion letters. (medium confidence)
25. 2 John — 2 John follows 1 John within the Johannine corpus. (medium confidence)
26. 3 John — 3 John follows the other Johannine letters. (medium confidence)
27. Revelation — Revelation closes the NT stream as the final canonical apocalypse; its precise date is debated. (medium confidence)
